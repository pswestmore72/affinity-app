THE CORE PHILOSOPHY

RUNNING AFFINITY

Affinity is a risk-attrition system. Players have Hit Dice. They spend them, lose them, and guard them. Your job: create situations where those dice matter.

There are no hit points. When you deal damage, you're destroying dice from dice pools. When an enemy has 5d8s and takes 3 damage, they now have 2d8s.

THE CORE TRUTH

Combat is fast. Resources deplete. Characters are fragile but capable.

Your first session should feel:
• TENSE (dice matter, choices have weight)
• FAIR (players understand the stakes)
• REWARDING (success feels earned)


QUICK ENCOUNTER BUILDING

FOR LEVEL 1 PARTIES (3-5 Players)

Easy: 8-12 HD total — 3-4 Trivial (1-2 HD)
Medium: 12-18 HD total — 2-3 Basic (3-5 HD)
Hard: 18-25 HD total — 1 Advanced + 2-3 Trivial
Deadly: 25+ HD total — 1 Advanced (6-10 HD) + multiple Basic

ENEMY ACTION BUDGET

Trivial: 1 action, 1-2 HD, RT 5
Basic: 1-2 actions, 3-5 HD, RT 5
Advanced: 1-2 actions, 6-10 HD, RT 4

At Level 1, avoid Expert+ enemies unless you want a very hard fight.

RUNNING COMBAT

COMBAT TURN STRUCTURE

1. ROLL INITIATIVE (everyone rolls 3d6, sum the total, highest first)
   • Range is 3-18; some NPCs roll fewer or more dice
   • Players CAN exert on initiative (if not surprised)
   • Ties: highest single die wins, then GM decides

2. EACH TURN: Move (10 yards free) + 2 actions

3. NPCS USE THEIR FIXED DICE POOLS (no exertion tracking)

WHEN NPCS ATTACK
1. Roll the dice shown in their stat block
2. Count successes at the listed tier (Basic 2+, Expert 4+, or Legendary 6; a 6 always = 2 successes)
3. If successes >= required, the attack hits — player defends

WHEN NPCS DEFEND
1. Player declares damage
2. NPC rolls Proficiency dice using their HD type
3. 5+ = sustain (die survives), 4- = die destroyed
4. NPC loses dice from their pool

NPC Simplification: NPCs don't burn HD on 1s. Just roll and move on.

FRIENDLY NPCS: Allied NPCs (reinforcements, companions) use the same rules — fixed dice pools, no exertion, no HD burn on 1s. Keep them simple so the players stay in the spotlight.

SWARM TIP: When running 4+ identical enemies, group them on one initiative count. Roll their attacks together and split damage across targets. This keeps swarm encounters fast.


MOVEMENT AND POSITIONING

Movement: 10 yards per turn (free, split however you like)

ENGAGEMENT: If you're in melee range (~5 yards) of an enemy and move away, that enemy makes a free strike against you — one Basic attack from their stat block. Costs them nothing. Happens every time.

To leave safely:
• Withdraw (costs 1 action): Move your speed, no free strikes
• Or just take the hit and run

POSITIONING BONUSES:
• Advantage (+1d6 to attacks): Favorable position — flanking, high ground, ambush, rear. GM calls it.
• Cover (+1d6 / +2d6 defense): Physical barrier. Light cover (bushes, furniture) = +1d6. Hard cover (walls, boulders) = +2d6.
• Concealment (+1d6 defense): Visual obscurity — fog, shadows, dense foliage. Distinct from cover; they stack.
• Brace (give up all movement): +1d6 to your next action

MOVEMENT ACTIONS:
• Charge (1 action + movement): Move 5+ yards straight, attack at +1d6
• Sprint (1 action): Double movement, can't attack
• Withdraw (1 action): Move safely out of engagement

WHY THIS MATTERS:
A Ranger in concealment deals bonus damage AND gets defense. A Weaver behind hard cover survives longer. A Combatant who Charges hits harder. Reward players who think about the battlefield — and make NPCs do the same.


DAMAGE AND DEATH

WHEN PLAYERS TAKE DAMAGE

1. Attacker declares HD damage
2. Player rolls defense (1 roll per attack at Defense Capacity 1)
3. 5+ on defense = that die survives, 1 damage cancelled
4. Failed rolls or excess damage = lose dice from pool (armor first, then body)

AT 0 BODY HD

• Character is still standing but LIMITED TO 1 ACTION per turn (body is breaking)
• Makes Resolve check each round automatically — no damage required, you're already breaking
• Fail = RT advances by 1 (2 > 3 > 4 > 5 > 6)
• If healed above 0 HD, regains 2 actions and stops Resolve checks
• At 0 HD AND Collapse (fail at RT 6) = dead

For Session 1: If a player hits 0 HD, remind them they only get 1 action per turn. Let them make one Resolve check, then have allies stabilize or heal them. Real death can come later when stakes are clear.


ARMOR RESTORATION (When Does Armor Come Back?)

Players WILL ask. Here's the answer:

Recover (6+ hours, safe place): Armor fully restored. Characters have time to mend, refit, and repair their gear.

Rest (30 min - 8 hours, threatened): Armor NOT restored. You're catching your breath in hostile territory, not running a forge. Body heals. Steel doesn't.

Field Repair (Craft check, Expert 4+, need 2 successes):
• Requires tools (smith's kit, leather repair kit, or equivalent)
• Takes 10 minutes of uninterrupted work
• Success: Full armor layer restored
• Failure: Armor stays broken — try again after next rest
• No tools = no repair. Period.

WHY THIS MATTERS:
Armor is the first line of defense. Once it's gone, every hit reaches Body HD — which means Resolve checks. Players who lose armor early feel the pressure fast. That's intentional. Let them scramble for a Recover or a Craft check. That tension is the game.


RESOLVE ESCALATION (When to Call for Checks)

Players make a Resolve check the first time each round that damage destroys a Body HD. Max 1 check per round.

THE KEY INSIGHT: If armor absorbs the whole hit, no Resolve check! Armor protects your mind as well as your body.

AT LEVEL 1 (What to Watch For):
• Any time damage gets through armor to Body HD = check
• At 0 HD, every hit that reaches body = check (automatic spiral)

RESULTS:
• Roll 1d6 >= current RT = Hold (no change)
• Roll below RT = RT increases by 1

THE TWO BREAKPOINTS (simple, no tracking):
• RT 5+: Cannot use Expert+ actions (mind is cracking)
• 0 HD: Limited to 1 action per turn (body is breaking)

That's it. No graduated penalty table. The escalating Resolve check is the spiral — each failed check makes the next one harder.

WHY THIS MATTERS AT LEVEL 1:
Players WILL face Resolve checks when they:
• Take a hit that gets past armor to Body HD
• Get reduced to 0 HD (now limited to 1 action!)
• Take ANY hit while at 0 HD (spiral accelerates)

Watch for damage reaching Body HD — that's your trigger!


SKILL CHALLENGES

RUNNING SKILL CHALLENGES

Affinity is a group game. Most skill challenges are resolved as GROUP ROLLS where everyone contributes and successes are pooled.

GROUP ROLL METHOD (Default)

1. Announce the domain: "This is an Agility challenge."
2. Everyone rolls: Each player rolls Proficiency + any exertion + domain bonuses
3. Pool successes: Add all successes together
4. Compare to threshold: Did the group total meet the required successes?

SETTING DIFFICULTY

At Level 1 with 3-4 players (Proficiency 1 each), you have a group dice pool of 3-4 dice.

QUICK DIFFICULTY GUIDELINES:
• EASY: Basic (2+), few successes needed (2)
• MODERATE: Basic (2+), more successes (3)
• HARD: Expert (4+), standard successes (2)
• VERY HARD: Expert (4+) with 3+ successes, or Legendary (6)

EXAMPLE CHALLENGES (for 3-4 player party):

Easy Wilderness Check: 2+, need 2 successes
Moderate Awareness: 2+, need 3 successes
Hard Climb: 4+, need 2 successes
Very Hard Lore: 4+, need 3 successes

Pip flavor: 2+ = anyone can help, 4+ = expertise required, 6 = only perfection counts

At Level 1, expect players to exert for harder challenges.

THE EIGHT DOMAINS

Agility — Stealth, acrobatics, movement
Awareness — Perception, insight, investigation
Fortitude — Endurance, resistance, stamina
Wilderness — Tracking, foraging, navigation
Influence — Persuasion, deception, intimidate
Lore — History, arcana, religion
Medicine — Healing, diagnosis, anatomy
Craft — Making, repairing, building

WHEN TO USE INDIVIDUAL ROLLS

• Initiative order actions (each character acts separately)
• Personal consequences (resisting poison, maintaining concentration)
• Spotlight moments (one player attempting something only they can do)
• GM discretion (when the fiction demands individual outcomes)

EMERGENCY GM FIXES

Fight too easy?
• Add reinforcements
• Give enemies +1d6 to attacks
• NPC uses desperate move

Fight too hard?
• Trivial/Basic foes flee at half HD
• Reinforcements arrive (for players)
• Environmental effect ends fight early

Player confused?
• "Roll d6s. Count successes by tier (usually 2+ at Level 1, and 6 = 2 successes)."
• "When hit, roll defense. 5+ = die survives."

Player at 0 HD?
• They get 1 action per turn (not 2)
• Make Resolve check each round (below RT = RT increases by 1)
• Stabilize action from ally stops spiral
• Don't kill players in Session 1 unless earned


You're Ready to Run

Trust the dice. Trust your players. The system is forgiving at Level 1 — small dice pools mean combat is fast, one defense roll per attack keeps it simple, and NPCs with 2-5 HD don't last long.
