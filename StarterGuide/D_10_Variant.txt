DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

This variant replaces d6 pool counting with a single d10 roll plus additive d6 bonuses. The result: cleaner resolution, margin-based effect gating, and preserved attrition through exertion burn.

Core shifts:
• One roll per action (not a pool)
• Proficiency d6s and Exertion d6s add face value to the d10
• Each ability has its own margin breakpoints (not universal "required successes")
• Natural 10 grants +1 margin
• Exertion d6s showing 1 still destroy HD (your signature cost)

Why this keeps Affinity:
• Exertion is still a blood decision
• Defense and Resolve systems untouched
• Margin-based scaling mirrors "layers of success"
• Attrition is front and center in ability design
• Feels fresh (D&D/DCC adjacent) but distinctly Affinity

HOW TO ROLL: ACTION RESOLUTION

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
THE CORE LOOP (ADDITIVE SYSTEM)

1. Declare what you're doing (ability name, if any)
2. Roll 1d10 + add all Proficiency d6s + any Exertion d6s
3. Compare total to the ability's Action Difficulty (AD)
4. Determine Margin = Total - AD
5. Consult ability text for margin breakpoints
6. Apply effects based on margin achieved
7. Resolve any exertion die 1s (each = 1 HD lost)

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
EXAMPLE: QUICK REFERENCE

You attack an Orc with Weapon Strike (a Basic ability, AD 7).

Roll: 1d10 = 6
Add: 1d6 (Prof 1) = 4
Add: 1d6 (Exert 1) = 3
Total: 6 + 4 + 3 = 13

Margin: 13 - 7 = +6

Weapon Strike base effect activates at Meeting AD (7+).
Margin +1 effect activates at 8+.
Margin +3 effect activates at 10+.
Margin +6+ effect activates at 13+.

You're at +6, so you hit base, +1, +3, and +6+ effects. Full escalation!

Check exertion die (rolled 3): Not a 1, so no HD burn. Lucky.

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
NATURAL 10 RULE

If your d10 shows 10 (before adding d6s): Add +1 to your margin.

Example: d10 rolls 10, adds to 15 total, margin is +8 instead of +7.

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
ACTION DIFFICULTY (AD) BY TIER

Level 1 baseline:

BASIC (AD 7): Routine actions, known targets, favorable conditions
EXPERT (AD 13): Trained skill, uncertain conditions, skilled opposition
LEGENDARY (AD 25): Mastery-level, dangerous, near-impossible for unprepared

These are starting points. GM adjusts by +/- 1-2 for circumstance.

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
EXERTION (THE COST)

• Add up to Proficiency exertion d6s to your action roll
• At Level 1: 1 exertion die per action
• Each exertion die showing 1: You lose 1 HD from your pool (before or after success doesn't matter)
• This cost is always taken, whether you hit the AD or not

The decision: Is the margin worth the blood?

DESIGNING ABILITIES WITH MARGIN BREAKS

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
TEMPLATE

[ABILITY NAME] (Tier, AD X)

BASE EFFECT (Meet AD):
• [What happens when you equal or exceed AD]

MARGIN +1 (At AD+1 or higher):
• [First enhancement or secondary benefit]

MARGIN +3 (At AD+3 or higher):
• [Significant escalation: damage boost, secondary target, special effect]

MARGIN +6+ (At AD+6 or higher):
• [Cascading: full combo, devastating bonus, or narrative flourish]

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
DESIGN PHILOSOPHY FOR MARGIN BREAKS

• **Base effect always matters**: Meeting AD should feel satisfying
• **+1 to +3 margin is "good"**: Incremental bonuses unlock as you roll better
• **+4 to +6 margin is "excellent"**: Secondary mechanics escalate
• **+7+ is "transcendent"**: Rare, high-skill, high-exertion moment
• **Abilities fill the range internally**: Basic abilities have breakpoints from 8-12; Expert from 14-24; Legendary from 26+

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
EXAMPLE: WEAPON STRIKE REIMAGINED

Weapon Strike (Basic, AD 7)

BASE EFFECT (Meet AD 7+):
• Roll succeeds, weapon hits target
• Light weapon: 1 HD damage
• Heavy weapon: 2 HD damage

MARGIN +1 (At 8+):
• Light weapon: 2 HD damage (upgraded)
• Heavy weapon: 3 HD damage (upgraded)

MARGIN +3 (At 10+):
• Light weapon: 2 HD damage + trigger weapon property
• Heavy weapon: 3 HD damage + trigger weapon property
• Attacker chooses property outcome (e.g., Swift = bonus attack; Crushing = knock prone; Precise = target -1d6 on next defense; Piercing = ignores armor)

MARGIN +6+ (At 13+):
• Damage + property + knockback 5 yards OR Resolve check on target (if body HD would be damaged)

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
EXAMPLE: HOLD THE LINE REIMAGINED

Hold the Line (Basic, AD 10)

BASE EFFECT (Meet AD 10+):
• Zone activates: 10-yard radius around you for Proficiency rounds
• Enemies in zone have movement halved
• Allies in zone gain +1d6 to their first defense each round (doesn't burn HD)

MARGIN +1 (At 11+):
• Zone radius increases to 15 yards

MARGIN +3 (At 13+):
• Zone radius 15 yards + allies gain +1d6 to ALL defenses in zone (not just first)

MARGIN +5 (At 15+):
• All previous + allies gain +1 to Proficiency until zone ends

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
WHY THIS DESIGN APPROACH

• **Abilities telegraph their payoff**: Players see margin breaks and think "do I want this?"
• **Exertion becomes tactical**: "If I exert, I can probably hit +2. Worth it? +4 would be amazing but risky."
• **Attrition is front-loaded**: Every exert d6 is a blood gamble before you even resolve
• **Margin becomes readable**: "I rolled 15 against AD 11, so I'm at +4. Full effect."

MOVEMENT AND POSITIONING (UNCHANGED)

Movement, engagement, advantage, cover, concealment, charge, withdraw: **All work exactly as before**. These don't use AD rolls.

When positioning grants "+1d6 to attacks," that d6 adds directly to your action total.

SKILL CHALLENGES (GROUP ROLLS)

Outside combat, skills still use group rolls where everyone rolls and pools successes.

Convert to additive system:
• Everyone rolls: 1d6 per Proficiency (or 1d6 + situational bonus)
• Pool all d6 totals
• Compare group total to threshold
• Thresholds for Level 1: Easy 8, Moderate 12, Hard 16, Very Hard 20

Example: 3-player party (Proficiency 1 each), Awareness check, Moderate threshold (12).

Each player rolls 1d6 (no d10, just the bonus dice). Total: 3 + 5 + 4 = 12. Success!

REVISED COMBAT EXAMPLE: KAEL VS ORC (d10 ADDITIVE)

Scene: Collapsed bridge, orc raider with raised axe.

Kael: 5d10s body, 2d8s armor, 1d12 shield, Proficiency 1
Orc: 4d8s body, 2d6s armor, Prof 2 (fixed, doesn't exert), AD modifier +1

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
ROUND 1 — KAEL ATTACKS

Kael uses Weapon Strike (Basic, AD 7) with a longsword (heavy weapon). He exerts.

Roll: 1d10 = 7
Prof d6 (1): 4
Exert d6 (1): 2
Total: 7 + 4 + 2 = 13

Margin: 13 - 7 = +6

Weapon Strike breakpoints:
• Meet AD (7+): Heavy weapon, 2 HD damage ✓
• Margin +1 (8+): Heavy weapon, 3 HD damage ✓
• Margin +3 (10+): 3 HD damage + property ✓
• Margin +6+ (13+): 3 HD damage + property + knockback ✓

Kael's exert die rolled 2 (no 1), so no HD burned.

Result: Orc takes 3 HD damage. Longsword property (Crushing) triggers: Orc rolls to resist knockback or is pushed 5 yards back.

Orc makes a Fortitude check (1d6, no exertion for NPCs). Rolls 3. Orc resists and stays put.

Orc is now: 4d8s body, 2d6s armor, taking 3 damage total = 1d8s and 2d6s (one body die destroyed).

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
ROUND 1 — ORC STRIKES BACK

Orc swings Greataxe (Basic, but has Prof 2 so uses their fixed pool).

NPC stat block: Greataxe attack at AD 7 (Basic NPC), rolls 3d6 fixed (Prof 2 gives 2 dice + 1 for axe bonus).

Roll: 3d6 = 6, 4, 2 = 12
Total for NPC: 12 (NPCs don't add a d10; they just roll fixed pool)
Orc AD for this attack: 7

Margin: 12 - 7 = +5

Orc's Greataxe breakpoints (NPC uses old-style fixed pool, but margins align similarly):
• Meet AD (7+): 2 HD damage ✓
• Margin +1-2 (8-9+): 3 HD damage ✓
• Margin +3+ (10+): 3 HD damage + knockdown ✓

Orc hits at +5 margin: 3 HD damage + knockdown attempt.

Kael defends: Rolls 1d8 (armor first) = 5. Success! 1 damage cancelled.
2 HD remains. Next die rolls 3 (fails). Second armor die destroyed.
Kael's armor: 2d8s → 0d8s (armor gone).
But no Body HD lost. No Resolve check.

Knockdown attempt: Kael rolls Fortitude check. 1d6 = 5. Resists! Stays on feet.

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
ROUND 2 — KAEL CONSIDERS HIS OPTIONS

Kael is healthy (5d10s body intact) but armor is gone. Next hit will reach body.

He could:
• Attack again with Weapon Strike (risky without armor)
• Use Oath-Driven Strike (Basic, AD 10, requires exert to hit +3 margin)
• Rally an ally (if there were one)
• Hold the Line (AD 10, defensive stance)

Kael chooses: **Oath-Driven Strike with exertion** (an ability requiring decision and blood).

Oath-Driven Strike (Basic, AD 10)
• Meet AD (10+): Weapon damage (2 HD for heavy) + Proficiency HD = 3 HD total
• Margin +1 (11+): 3 HD + knock prone
• Margin +3 (13+): 3 HD + prone + Resolve check on target

Roll: 1d10 = 8
Prof d6 (1): 3
Exert d6 (1): 1 ← BURNED HD!
Total: 8 + 3 + 1 = 12

Margin: 12 - 10 = +2 (meets +1 margin!)

Kael hits at +2 margin: Base effect (3 HD damage) + margin +1 effect (knock prone).

But exert die rolled 1: **Kael loses 1 Body HD**. Now 4d10s (was 5).

This triggers a Resolve check! Kael's RT is 2. Rolls 1d6 = 6. Holds! (needs 2+).

Damage: 3 HD to Orc.
Orc defends: Rolls 2d8 (body now, armor is gone) = 6, 4. Both succeed! 2 damage cancelled.
1 HD remains: Orc loses 1d8.

Orc is now: 3d8s body, 0d6 armor. Below half HP. Rage triggers (Orc ability): +1d6 to next attack.

Prone effect: Orc is on the ground. Next attack from Orc has disadvantage (GM reduces their effective roll by 1 margin).

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
ROUND 2 — ORC RETALIATES (PRONE, BUT RAGING)

Orc stands (costs movement). Swings Greataxe with rage bonus.

Normally 3d6, now 4d6 due to rage (GM adds 1d6 bonus). But prone doesn't apply yet (stood up).

Roll: 4d6 = 5, 5, 3, 2 = 15 (very hot roll!)
Orc's attack AD is still 7.

Margin: 15 - 7 = +8

Damage: 3 HD + all breakpoints trigger + knockback + Resolve check.

Kael defends: Rolls 1d10 (body, heavy die) = 8. Success! 1 damage cancelled.
2 HD reach body. Next roll 1d10 = 4 (fails). 1d10 destroyed.

Kael: 4d10s → 3d10s. Resolve check! Rolls 1d6 = 3. Holds (needs 2+, no change).

Orc's knockback: Kael pushed 5 yards (or knocked prone). Kael chooses to take knockback. No engagement break.

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
ROUND 3 — FINAL EXCHANGE

Kael is at 3d10s body, no armor, RT 2. Orc is at 3d8s body, prone, no armor.

Kael uses simple Weapon Strike (no exert this time—too risky).

Roll: 1d10 = 9
Prof d6 (1): 5
Total: 9 + 5 = 14

AD 7, Margin = +7

Full Weapon Strike + property + knockback (Orc is already down, so prone effect extends duration).

3 HD damage. Orc defends: 2d8 = 7, 3. One success, one fail. 1 damage cancelled, 1 die destroyed.
2 HD remains: Both fail. Orc loses both remaining d8s.

**Orc is at 0 Body HD. Unconscious.**

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
AFTERMATH

Kael stands at 3d10s body, no armor, RT 2. Bruised, bloodied, barely victorious.

The exertion gamble in Round 2 paid off: he hit Oath-Driven Strike at +3 margin (knocked Orc prone, set up for finish) but paid 1 HD.

Without exertion, he wouldn't have landed the precision strike that turned the fight. With exertion, the fight lasted 3 rounds instead of 5. **The choice was real.**

CRITICAL DESIGN NOTES

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
WHAT STAYS THE SAME
• Defense (d4, d6, d8, d10, d12 defending; 5+ survives)
• Resolve (Threshold starts 2, escalates on failed checks)
• Armor destruction (layers: armor → body)
• Movement, engagement, positioning (free 10 yards, cost to disengage)
• Hit Dice depletion (actual dice lost, not HP subtracted)

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
WHAT CHANGES
• Single d10 + additive d6s (no pool counting)
• Margin-based ability effects (not required successes)
• Each ability has unique breakpoints (not universal)
• Exertion d6s add face value to total (not separate pool)

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
WHY THIS PRESERVES AFFINITY
• Exertion still burns HD (1s on exert dice)
• Attrition is central to every action
• Defense/Resolve untouched (core tension preserved)
• Margin concept mirrors "degrees of success" from pools
• Single die feels fresh but system is fundamentally about scarcity and choice

{H3: Oswald Bold 18pt}
WHY THIS FEELS ACCESSIBLE
• One die to read (familiar to D&D, DCC players)
• Additive math is intuitive
• Margin language is clear ("You're +3, so X happens")
• Exertion decision is obvious ("Do I pay 1 HD for +1d6?")
• Faster resolution (single roll + margin lookup vs. pool counting)

CHANDLE'S ASIDE: ON THE d10 ADDITIVE SHIFT

"I watched playtests of this system, and here's what I saw: Players understood the mechanic in two sentences. No 'wait, does a 6 equal two successes?' confusion. They rolled, added their d6s, checked the margin, and made a real choice: exert or hold back?

That choice—that moment of 'my next d6 might burn a Hit Die'—is where Affinity lives. The d10 doesn't dilute it. It sharpens it. Every exertion is a negotiation with mortality.

And the margin breakpoints? That's where the designers got clever. A Weaver doesn't have the same 3+ triggers as a Combatant. Each ability breathes. Each tells a story in its breakpoints.

This feels like Affinity because it keeps what makes Affinity hard: nothing is free. You pay in dice or you don't get the moment. That's still the game."

— Chandle
