AFFINITY: FRACTURE
AERALON: A WORLD PRIMER
The Weave and the Realms Beyond


"You want to know about the world before you walk into it. Smart. Most adventurers skip this part — and most adventurers die surprised. What follows is history, warning, and something in between. All of it is true, or close enough that the difference won't save you."

— Chandle, Chandler Services of Generalities and Oddities


WELCOME TO AERALON

The gods broke the world arguing over who should fix it. The dead remained — bound by grief, duty, or simply forgotten. The forests remember what the gods would rather you didn't.

Aeralon is a high-fantasy cosmology built on a single principle:

BALANCE IS NOT PEACE. BALANCE IS TENSION.

The cosmos holds together not because its forces cooperate, but because they push against each other so hard that nothing can move without consequence.

That tension has a name: THE FRACTURE.

Not one fracture — all of them. The cosmic scar left when one god rewrote reality. The hairline cracks where divine realms bleed into mortal ground. The fractures inside every person who carries competing loyalties, contradictory oaths, and impossible choices.

The cosmos is fractured. So are the people who live in it.

You live in the Mortal Weave — the largest single focal point for all of these forces. Every god reaches toward your world. None may claim it. That contested space is where your stories happen.


THE SHAPE OF THE COSMOS

All existence is suspended within the Veil Beyond — a lightless boundary separating reality from unreality. Nothing exists beyond it. Nothing can.

Within the Veil drift the Realms, vast territories shaped by belief, principle, and primordial force.

Between them flows the Ethereal Void — navigable emptiness measured not in distance, but in will and intent.

THE THREE GREAT STRATA

THE REFLECTED REALMS — Three worlds bound to the others
THE SOVEREIGN REALMS — Seats of divine authority and principle
THE FRACTURED REALMS — Unstable, lawless, or forbidden spaces


THE REFLECTED REALMS

Three echoes of a shared truth — mortality, growth, and ending — bound together in a reflection that cannot be broken without unmaking all three.

The Weave, the Gloam, and the Verge are distinct worlds with their own landscapes, inhabitants, and laws. But they cannot exist without each other.

Mortality needs endings. Endings need new growth. Growth needs something to grow from. Pull one away and the other two collapse — not slowly, not eventually, but immediately. The Sovereign Realms hold enough divine power to stand alone. The Fractured Realms are what happens when separation is forced — reality torn from itself, scarred into something that cannot heal. These three have never separated. The cosmos will not allow it.

Together they form a prime focal point of mortal will, divine power, cosmic law, and arcane energy.


THE WEAVE (The Mortal Weave)
Realm of Balance, Memory, and Mortality

This is your world. The core Plane of existence. The only Plane to encompass elements and inhabitants from all other realms. Full of life and death in near equal measure — and this balance makes it unique.

Every god reaches toward the Mortal Weave, yet none may claim it. The Balance exists because they cannot agree.

Crownstags wander ancient forests. River-serpents guard waterways older than kingdoms. Glass-winged hawks hunt at altitudes where the Veil thins. Energies migrate here from every surrounding Plane — and mortals are the only beings trusted to hold the tension by the will of the cosmos.

No being of higher power is allowed entrance into the Mortal Weave; higher beings routinely bypass these restrictions. But only one deity resides here freely: Aluneth, the great Dragon Turtle of memory and tides, whose deep waters no one has found twice.


THE GLOAM (The Hollow Marches)
Realm of Death, Endings, and Mercy

Shrouded in haze and pale darkness — lit only by dim ever-present moons and faint distant stars. The Gloam is not where the dead go. It is where endings accumulate. Every life that concludes, every kingdom that falls, every fire that gutters out — the weight of it settles here.

It is loss given geography, grief made tangible, and mercy made final.

The Gloam does not have seasons or governments. It has STATES OF MOURNING that roll across the landscape like weather:

THE VIGIL — Denial made manifest. The recently dead walk beside the living, unaware anything has changed.
THE REMEMBRANCE — Grief that honors. The dead appear as they wished to be remembered. Monuments rise from nothing.
THE RELEASE — Acceptance. The exhale. Mists thin, paths open, and the dead finally pass onward.
THE HOLLOW — Loss curdled into emptiness. The dead do not appear — they hunger. Names are forgotten.

Vampires, ghosts, revenants — most are not hostile by nature. They are simply unresolved. The truly dangerous are those who have forgotten why they remain.


THE VERGE (The Tangled Verge)
Realm of Excess Life, Growth, and Wild Magic

Where the Gloam is endings given weight, the Verge is beginnings that never learned to stop. Unchecked vitality. Biological excess. Generative chaos so dense that the air itself breeds. Despite its beauty, this is one of the most dangerous planes in existence — not because it is hostile, but because it does not recognize the difference between feeding you and consuming you.

This is not a kingdom. It is an ecosystem that achieved consciousness — and still never stopped.

The Verge does not have rulers. It has ECOLOGICAL IMPERATIVES — biomes that achieved sentience, rolling through the realm in century-long waves like seasons that think:

THE BLOOM — Unstoppable growth. Forests overtake mountains in hours. Beauty becomes suffocation. Step off the path and the path grows over.
THE WITHERING — Sentient decay. Everything falls. Nothing new grows until the old is composted into memory. The Verge digesting itself.
THE BURIED ROOT — Old growth thinking in centuries. Trees become stone-hard. Roots crack bedrock. Nothing young breaks through. Nothing young is allowed to.
THE TEMPEST — Creative violence. Storms seed forests instantly. Lightning births new species that live, fight, and die before the thunder fades.

The Verge is what life looks like when death has no say. This is why the three Reflected Realms cannot separate — without the Gloam to end what the Verge begins, growth becomes cancer. Without the Weave to hold the line between them, both fracture.


THE SOVEREIGN REALMS

Seats of divine authority where the gods maintain their highest power. Filled with divine magic — similar to arcane magic but more difficult to wield, often requiring aid from stronger beings.

THE EMPYREAL ACCORD
Realm of Law, Unity, and Sacred Obligation

The largest Outer Plane — and the heaviest. Not in mass, but in expectation. The air here carries the weight of every oath ever sworn in good faith. Light does not illuminate — it judges. Celestials walk these halls in forms that mortals find beautiful only because the alternative would be madness: oath-lions whose manes burn with the color of promises kept, silvered serpents that taste dishonesty on the wind, pegasi whose wingbeats sound like gavels falling.

This is where the gods of light hold their divine seats. Nyroth Stonebound. Aubret the Unbent. Beryn the Laughing Dawn. Caerith the Dawnwarden. Korthane the Coinbound. They do not agree with each other. They agree that the law must hold — and disagree on every detail of what that means.

Travel here is not advised. Not because the Accord is hostile, but because it holds visitors to the same standard it holds its gods. Mortals who enter carrying broken oaths have been known to simply stop existing — not killed, not judged, just quietly un-recognized by a realm that cannot perceive the faithless.


THE WILD EXPANSE OF THEIWANAR
Realm of Titanic Life and Perfect Nature

Imagine nature with nothing to fear. No cities to avoid. No mortals to accommodate. No divine politics to navigate. Just life — pursuing its own logic at scales that make mortal ecosystems feel like terrariums.

Vast mountain ranges where the peaks are living coral. Oceans that think in currents. Frigid tundras where the ice remembers every creature that ever crossed it. A rainforest spanning forty percent of an entire world's mass, so dense that its canopy has developed its own weather, its own gravity, its own sense of time. Home to Thymera the Verdant-Mother (Goddess of Life) and Darthun the Coil Below (Lord of Decay) — the oldest argument in the cosmos, sharing a realm because neither can exist without the other.

God-apes that speak in subsonic frequencies felt in the marrow. Bloom-hydras whose severed heads grow into new ecosystems. Thunder-tortoises whose shells carry forests older than most civilizations.

Open to travel. Extreme caution advised — not because the Expanse is hostile, but because it does not notice you. A creature the size of a mountain range does not avoid foot traffic. It does not know foot traffic exists.


THE ENGINE OF BAEL-ZAROTH
Realm of Obligation and Tyranny

Also known as The Final Obligation, The Depth, or simply The Hells. This is not a kingdom. It is a malfunctioning metaphysical machine.

Before the Infernal Schism, this realm was a balanced engine of consequence governed by multiple powers. Then Azravael performed the Crown-Breaking — an act that replaced the final line of every contract in existence. All debt, all obligation, all binding now terminates in her.

She did not conquer. She rewrote the fundamental nature of obligation.

The deeper you go, the less free will matters — until existence itself constitutes agreement. Entry is forbidden not because the realm is dangerous (though it is), but because entering constitutes acceptance of local jurisdiction. The Engine interprets presence as consent.

Even speaking Azravael's name too often can trigger micro-obligations that scar one's soul.


THE FRACTURED REALMS

These were realms once. Whole places with their own balance, their own logic, their own peoples.

Then the gods went to war unchecked. No Custodial Power held the line. No mortal hands steadied the tension. Divine force met divine force and reality lost. What remains are the scars — planes where the fundamental rules of existence cracked and never healed. Reality still tries to function here. It does not succeed cleanly.

The Reflected Realms cannot separate because the cosmos will not allow it. The Fractured Realms are proof of what happens when it does.


THE ASHEN WASTE (The Wastelands)
Realm of Pain and Suffering as Truth

Once a place of sensation in balance — pleasure and pain in dialogue, feeling as a means of understanding. The divine conflict that fractured it burned away every middle register. What remains is a vast unlivable desert where only extremes survive. The ruins of older civilizations jut from the sand — arches that once framed gardens, foundations of amphitheaters where music played. Now the wind through them sounds like screaming because the acoustics were designed for a world that no longer exists.

Gephorix the Many-Masked rules here — deity of excess, sensation, and truth revealed through extremity. He did not break this realm. He is what grew from the break. Most inhabitants live underground. The portal distance to the Mortal Weave is the shortest of any Outer Plane, making it a jumping point for illegal planar travel.

Gephorix does not distinguish between agony and bliss. Both are merely intensity. And intensity reveals what moderation conceals.


THE ROILING CRUCIBLE
Realm of Primordial Flux and Elemental Becoming

Once the foundational forge — where raw elements were shaped into the building blocks that every other realm used to construct itself. The divine wars shattered its organizing principle. Now the elements no longer combine in stable patterns. They crash, merge, separate, and recombine in endless violent flux. Occasionally something coherent forms — a landscape, an atmosphere, a moment of stillness — before the next wave unmakes it.

The Plane of Elements — all elements found on any Plane exist here, but none stay fixed. Central to the Pact of Neutrality, which bars all higher beings from using it as a gateway. Iosyx the Twin-Flame (Chaos, Elements, Change) and Zarun the Unshaped (Freedom, Identity, Becoming) call this home — two gods who found in the wreckage something that suited them.

Closed to general travel. Expeditions require authorization from the Curator Arcanum. Most do not return with the same number they departed with.


THE OUTER BLACK (Eternal Dark)
Realm of Absence and Unmaking

The worst wound. Whatever this realm was before the fracture, it is gone so completely that even the memory of it has been unmade. No ruins. No echoes. No archaeological evidence that anything ever existed here. Just deep, black, empty space — and the things that live in absence.

The Outer Black is what happens when a fracture goes all the way through. Not broken — erased. Reality does not struggle here. Reality gave up.

Its most powerful inhabitants are simply called the Nameless. None have been seen. Only known encounters come from planar meddling of fools. Creatures brought from here are among the worst terrors released onto any Plane.

ILLEGAL TO TRAVEL. Some domains will detain or kill anyone caught traveling to or from this Plane on sight.


GODS IN CONFLICT

These are not superheroes with portfolios. They are principles that achieved personality — and principles do not share power gracefully.

No god is safe to worship. Even the righteous ones war with each other. Even mercy has enemies among the merciful. The cosmos fractures along these fault lines — and mortals choose which side of the crack they stand on.


THE WAR OF DEBTS — Korthane and Azravael

Korthane the Coinbound says all debts must end. His temples are counting houses and neutral ground where even enemies can trade. Fair exchange. Finite obligation.

Azravael says debts never end. She rewrote reality so every contract in existence terminates in her. She does not need worship — only that you owe something to someone.

Both claim dominion over what people owe. Every contract signed in the Mortal Weave is contested ground between them.

THE SHIELD AND THE LINE — Caerith and Aubret

Caerith the Dawnwarden holds the line so others may flee. Mercy made active — she will break precedent to save the innocent.

Aubret the Unbent is law made absolute. A vertical silver line — unbending, uncompromising. Never once entered the Mortal Weave. The law must hold. No exceptions.

Both righteous. Both necessary. When the law demands a sacrifice that mercy finds monstrous, kingdoms shatter between them.

THE ANVIL AND THE SHAPESHIFTER — Nyroth and Zarun

Nyroth Stonebound is a great Dwarf with a hammer and clasped hands. Fighting, friendship, honor. He will answer when called — but only to those who keep their bonds.

Zarun the Unshaped is constantly shifting form. Freedom, identity, transformation. No promise should chain what you are becoming.

What does a soul owe to the person it used to be? Followers of Nyroth say everything. Followers of Zarun say nothing.

THE MOTHER AND THE SERPENT — Thymera and Darthun

Thymera the Verdant-Mother is the Goddess of all Life. Nature, growth, healing, inevitability. A common Elf in wreaths of greenery and flowers who does not distinguish between the weed and the rose.

Darthun the Coil Below is an immense python. Poison, disease, rot, necessity. Seen as evil because his worshipers often are — but decay is not malice. It is maintenance.

They share a realm. They need each other. Life that refuses to end becomes cancer. Death that refuses to feed becomes void. They are the oldest remaining argument in the cosmos.

THE DEATH WHO CHOSE THE DARK — Aralys

Aralys the Veilbearer — a Moon Elf with dark purple skin and bright white hair. Death, final mercy, remembrance. She chose to wander the Gloam while other gods remain seated on their thrones.

Even she is dangerous. The final mercy she offers is not always desired by those who receive it.

THE COMFORT AND THE PAIN — Ilyra and Gephorix

Ilyra the Hearthbound never manifests physically. You will not see her. You will feel the room change — the fire pulling closer, the silence between friends becoming something that heals instead of aches. Worshiped in kitchens, inns, and rebuilt villages. Her temples are any place where someone sets a second plate at the table just in case. She asks nothing of her faithful except that they remain open to the possibility that someone nearby is suffering quietly and could use a warm meal.

Gephorix the Many-Masked wears three heads: the weeping face (suffering), the laughing face (pleasure), the silent face (desire unfulfilled). Where Ilyra is the pause, Gephorix is the sensation that makes the pause necessary. He does not distinguish between agony and bliss — both are intensity, and intensity is the only honest register. His followers do not seek pain. They seek the truth that only extremity reveals.

THE DAWN AND THE WHISPER — Beryn and Velkir

Beryn the Laughing Dawn is joy made radiant — a young god who sings at the edge of every sunrise. Art, celebration, the truth told in the open where everyone can hear it. His temples are stages, festivals, and any place where laughter breaks silence. He believes that truth shared freely loses its power to harm.

Velkir the Ash-Tongued is his shadow. Secrets, corruption, and whispered power. Truths stripped of context. Power without perspective. What happens when knowledge loses humility. His temples are empty rooms where someone left a note.

Beryn says: speak everything, hide nothing, and truth cannot be weaponized. Velkir says: every truth has a knife edge, and the fool who shouts it in daylight cuts everyone nearby. The Curator Arcanum watches them both — because knowledge shouted recklessly and knowledge whispered selfishly both end the same way.

THE QUESTION — Aluneth

A Dragon Turtle roughly 60 yards long. Memory, tides, continuity. The only higher being legally residing in the Mortal Weave — and the only god who answers when asked. What she answers is never what you wanted. It is always what you needed. Her full story is told later in these pages, in a place where it can breathe.


THE CUSTODIAL POWERS

Every realm has custodial structures — forces that maintain local order so the plane does not tear itself apart. The Gloam has the Black Reliquary and the Loom of Fates. The Verge has the Verdant Synod and the Wheel of Ruin. The Empyreal Accord has Angelic hierarchies. Even the Engine of Bael-Zaroth has the Auditor Choir and the Amendment Scriveners.

Each of these serves its realm. Often they serve its deities directly.

The Weave has three Custodial Powers. They are fundamentally different.

They serve no deity. They serve no realm. They serve Balance itself — the structure that allows gods to exist, that holds the cosmos together, that prevents the next fracture. Their jurisdiction is not local. It is cosmic. Their principles operate in every realm, across every plane, regardless of who rules there.

This is why the Fractured Realms broke. No custodial power held the line when the gods went to war. The Weave's three exist to ensure that never happens again — or at least, to stand in the way when the gods try again.

They are not governed by thrones. They are not institutions with buildings and budgets. They are paths — carved by the first mortals who chose to hold a tension that should have destroyed them, and walked by every generation since. Each time someone commits to the same principle and survives the weight of it, the path deepens. Each time someone walks it further than the last, the cosmos notices. Over millennia, those paths gained enough weight to shape reality itself. They became agreements made durable through sacrifice — not written in law, but worn into the structure of existence like water wears stone.

WHY MORTALS?

Deities embody absolutes. Azravael cannot forgive a debt without ceasing to be Azravael. Thymera cannot stop growth without becoming something else. Gephorix cannot relieve suffering without dissolving. Gods cannot hold contradictions — they shatter.

Mortals hold contradictions daily. A mortal can protect someone they know deserves punishment. Cultivate life while preparing to destroy it. Seek knowledge while keeping secrets. The Weave is the only realm where all cosmic pressures meet as equals — no single principle dominates, no god rules unopposed. The Balance exists because mortals can hold the tension that would break the gods.

This is also why these three accept members from anywhere in the cosmos. Celestials, spirits, mortal soldiers, scholars from the Gloam, rangers from the Verge — any being of any origin and any power level can walk these paths if they commit to holding the tension. The cosmos does not care where you come from. It cares whether you can hold the Balance.


THE CONCORDANT HALLS — Oath and Purpose

An oath alone is hollow. Purpose without oath is chaos. The Concordant Halls exist where those two forces meet.

Who walks the Halls: Paladins whose oaths define their identity. Fighters who carry codes older than the banners they serve. Judges, wardens, shield-captains, and civic guardians. The Concordant Halls can enforce oaths made in the Engine itself — their authority does not stop at planar borders.

Seven Charges govern their paths — the Shield, the Blade, the Bond, the Crown, the Flame, the Stone, the Bell — and none are moral. A paladin of light and an executioner of darkness can both walk the Halls if their oaths are absolute.

The cosmos does not judge the oath — only whether it is kept. This is why the Halls terrify the gods.


THE LIVING ACCORD — Life and Death

The Accord tells that life without death is stagnation, and death without life is annihilation.

Who bears the Accord: Rangers who understand when protection becomes harm. Druids who prune as often as they nurture. Shamans who speak for land, ancestors, and endings. The Living Accord can trigger ecological collapse in realms that refuse necessary endings — their reach extends into the Verge, the Wild Expanse, even the Ashen Waste.

Seven Living Domains govern their work — Breath, Thorn, Tide, Rot, Hearth, Star, Howl — and none are moral. The Living Accord acts when growth becomes cancerous, when preservation becomes hoarding, or when destruction exceeds renewal.


THE CURATOR ARCANUM — Knowledge and Humility

The Curator Arcanum exist because of a single, brutal truth: Knowledge without humility always ends the world.

Who walks these Paths: Scholars, weavers, arcanists, seekers. Those willing to learn that the most dangerous spell is the one you cast without understanding why. The Curator Arcanum can seal knowledge that even deities cannot uncover — and they have.

Six Paths govern their study — Moments, Thresholds, Unleashed Force, Mutable Flesh, Open Doors, Veiled Sight — and none are moral. There was a seventh. No one remembers what it governed. When the Outer Black was unmade, this knowledge was unmade with it — erased so completely that only the hole it left behind confirms it ever existed. Curator scholars call it the Absent Seat. They do not study it. They study the shape of its absence.

These three are not the good guys. A priest of Gephorix channels the Living Accord through suffering-as-transformation. A combatant sworn to Azravael walks the Concordant Halls through contract-enforcement-as-purpose. A scholar who studied the Outer Black serves the Curator Arcanum by knowing what must stay veiled.

The cosmos does not reward virtue. It rewards commitment, balance, and willingness to hold what cannot coexist.

Your character walks one of these paths. That is what Affinity: Fracture is about.


STORIES FROM AERALON

These fragments are drawn from the living history of the cosmos. The kinds of stories your characters might hear in taverns, argue about in forbidden libraries, or discover firsthand — usually at the worst possible moment.


THE THIRTY-EIGHTH CONGREGATION
(From the testimony of Chronicler Vedris Halfmoon, Curator Arcanum)

"The thing you need to understand — and I say this knowing full well that understanding will not stop you — is that we knew people were going to die. Not might. Not could. Would. In quantities that make 'party casualty' feel like a category error. We went anyway."

Every 38 and two-thirds years, the Ashen Waste tears open portals across every Plane. Not invitations — wounds. The desert bleeds light for three days before the Congregation begins, and by then tens of thousands are already walking through.

What follows is a twenty-day rite of intensity that Gephorix the Many-Masked did not merely tolerate — he ordained it as the highest sacrament of his faith. The air tastes like copper and burnt sugar. The music, if you can call it that, is played on instruments that require the performer to be in pain. The dancing does not stop. It cannot stop. Few participants remember whether the dancing is voluntary by hour six.

"A man in mismatched armor stumbled past me, grinning with teeth filed to points. 'First time?' he asked. 'You'll love it. Or you won't survive it. Both feel the same by hour three.'"

Survival rate: approximately 43%. The desert keeps no graves. What the sand covers, no one retrieves.

And yet the priestesses of Ilyra set up camp in the middle of it. Cool water. Actual shade. The simple miracle of someone willing to listen while the horizon screams.

"We are the breath between screams," one priestess told me, pouring tea from a pot that should not have been possible to carry across four Planes. "The pause that makes the next sensation mean something. Without comfort, intensity loses its contrast. Without hope, despair has no weight."

"Half these people came because they have nothing left to lose. The other half came because they've never lost anything and wanted to know what it felt like. Ilyra doesn't judge them. She serves. She comforts. That's the whole of her teaching — that comfort isn't weakness, that community isn't cowardice, that sometimes the most radical act of faith is not pursuing the limits of transcendence."

Gephorix and Ilyra, sharing the same desert. Intensity and comfort, side by side. Neither able to claim the whole truth. The priestess offered me a cup of tea. Behind her, someone was screaming in a register I did not know the mortal throat could produce. She did not flinch. She poured a second cup, in case I needed it.

I did.


THE CROWN-BREAKING

No one speaks the full name of what Azravael did. It is referred to only as the Crown-Breaking — the moment one will rewrote reality.

The survivors describe it differently depending on where they were standing. In the Engine, they say the air changed mid-sentence — that they were speaking one truth and by the end of the same breath it was a different truth, and had always been. On the Mortal Weave, contracts rewrote themselves. Ink moved on signed documents. Names appeared in ledgers that had been blank for centuries. A woman in a trade hall looked down at her own signature and did not recognize it, because it was no longer hers.

In between — at the fracture points, where reality thinned as infernal law propagated through the bones of existence — people changed. Not died. Changed. Mid-step. Mid-word. A merchant in the Engine's outer courts was filing a trade dispute when the Crown-Breaking rippled through her. Her left hand is still writing the original complaint. Her right hand has been writing Azravael's amendments for four hundred years. She does not notice the contradiction. She cannot.

These are the Schism-Scarred. Not a race. Not a curse. Living proof that one will can rewrite reality without the courtesy of destroying it first.

In the aftermath, every realm — mortal, divine, dead, and empty — agreed on exactly one thing: it must never happen again. They built the Threshold. A coalition force drawn from every Plane. Concordant warriors serve alongside Gloam shepherds. Curator scholars share intelligence with Celestial observers. Mortal soldiers stand guard beside spirits who remember the sound reality made when it rewrote itself. Every power has a seat — and every power has a spy. Where the Fracture bleeds through, the Threshold stands. Barely.

Azravael does not need you to worship her. She only needs you to owe something to someone. Check your contracts. Check the promises you made that you thought were metaphorical.

She does not think anything is metaphorical.


THE QUESTION OF ALUNETH

Aluneth dwells in the waters of the Weave — the only higher being legally residing there. Her domains are memory, tides, and continuity.

She will offer aid to any creature that asks, usually by answering a question.

No one has found her twice.

This is not poetry. This is a logistical fact that has driven navigation guilds to collective madness. She does not hide. She is 60 yards long. Sailors have mapped her wake, tracked her tidal patterns, studied lunar cycles, fed decades of navigational data into the best scrying engines the Curator Arcanum can build. She is simply not there when you go back. She was never there. The coordinates are correct. The water remembers nothing.

Those who find her report one consistent detail: she did not give them the answer they sought. She gave them the answer they needed. Most do not enjoy this experience. One sailor asked for the location of a sunken treasure and received, instead, his dead father's opinion of him. He wept for three days on an open sea that had gone suddenly, impossibly still.

Among her clergy, deliberate historical revision is considered a grave spiritual offense. Under the full moon, names of those lost at sea must be spoken aloud annually — or risk fading from communal memory. This is not ritual. This is maintenance. Memory is the only thing between existence and the Outer Black.

"Mortality remembers nothing forever." She said this to a child who asked if the world would end. The child — who later became the first Chronicler of the Curator Arcanum — spent three hundred and seventy years trying to prove her wrong, and she could not.


SEEDS FOR YOUR TABLE

The cosmos fractures along divine fault lines. But the cracks reach all the way down — into villages, families, and the quiet places where mortal lives intersect with forces too large to name. These hooks are where the tension meets the ground.


THE GOOD DOCTOR

The village healer has been curing everything. Broken bones mend in hours. Fevers break mid-sentence. A child born still started breathing when he touched it. He charges nothing. He sleeps less and less. His hair has gone white in three weeks. When pressed, he admits he found something in the woods — a snake, dead, coiled around a root that was still growing through its body. He picked it up and it dissolved into his hands. He has been healing since. He cannot stop. A farmer came to him with a lame horse and he fixed it, but the crops around the farmhouse has not grown since. His patients are getting better. Something nearby is getting worse. No one wants him to stop. The healer is not sure he can.

THE BUILDER'S OATH

A stonemason died mid-project — half a bridge, one side of the river. His apprentice finished it. Good work. Strong stone. But the bridge hums at night. Not the wind through it — the stone itself. People who cross it at dawn say they feel watched. Last week, the apprentice's hands started moving in her sleep, shaping stone that isn't there. She wakes with dust under her fingernails and no memory. The mason swore he'd finish the bridge. He did not finish the bridge. Something is trying to make good on the promise, and it is using the closest pair of hands it can find. The apprentice does not want to stop crossing. She says it feels like he's still teaching her.

THE ORCHARD

A family orchard on the edge of town has started producing fruit out of season. Apples in winter. Cherries in the frost. The fruit is perfect — the best anyone has tasted. The family is thrilled. They are selling it at market and people are lining up. No one has noticed yet that the tree line behind the orchard has moved forty feet closer to the house since last month. The eldest daughter has. She hasn't told anyone because the trees whisper to her at night, and what they say is not threatening. It is an invitation. They want her to step closer. They want to welcome her the way soil welcomes a seed.

THE LETTER

A dead woman's belongings arrive at the inn — shipped from a city two weeks' travel away, addressed to no one in particular. Inside: clothes, a ring, three books in a language the innkeeper can't read, and a sealed letter. The letter says, in plain common: "I need someone I have never met to do something I cannot explain. You will know what it is when the time comes. I am sorry for what that will cost you. I have left money." There is no money in the belongings. There is, however, a key that does not fit any lock in town — but it gets warm when carried near the old church yard. The innkeeper has set the whole box on the bar and not touched it since. No one has claimed it. No one has left.

THE FESTIVAL

A nearby village holds an annual festival — three days of music, market stalls, and a traditional dance nobody remembers the origin of. This year, the musicians say the tune has changed. Not dramatically — a note here, a rhythm there. They didn't choose to change it. Their fingers played differently and it sounded right. The dance has changed too. New steps that no one taught but everyone knows. The festival-goers are having the time of their lives. Outsiders who watch the dance feel vaguely nauseous and cannot explain why. A visiting priestess of Aralys has quietly asked the party to stay through the third night. She will not say what she expects to happen. She has, however, hired a cart large enough to carry several people who cannot walk.


MAKING AERALON YOURS

This is Aeralon. None of it is safe. All of it is yours.

The full cosmology contains detailed planar guides, expanded deity profiles, and mythology that stretches back to the moment before the Balance. But you do not need any of that to begin. You need one principle and one question.

The principle: BALANCE IS NOT PEACE. BALANCE IS TENSION.

The question: What does your character do when the tension finds them?

Because it will find them. A contract they did not sign will come due. A death they did not cause will become their road. A forest will offer them friendship in a language that has no word for refusal. A god will notice them — not because they are special, but because they are mortal, and mortals can hold what gods cannot.

The cosmos is fractured. Your character carries those fractures — competing loyalties, contradictory oaths, impossible choices that do not resolve cleanly. The world cracks along the same fault lines they do.

What they do with that pressure is your story.

This is Affinity: Fracture.

Your health is actual dice that get destroyed. Every action risks your survival. Mortality is not a statistic — it is a feeling. The same tension that holds the cosmos together holds your character sheet together, and both can shatter.

The Player's Starter Guide will teach you how to build a character who can hold the tension. The Game Master's Starter Guide will teach you how to break them.

This primer taught you why it all matters.

Welcome to Aeralon. The Balance holds — for now.
