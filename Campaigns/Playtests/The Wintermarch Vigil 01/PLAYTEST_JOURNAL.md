# The Wintermarch Vigil — Playtest Journal

**Playtest ID:** The Wintermarch Vigil 01  
**Party:** Tormund (Combatant), Kessa (Combatant), Sera (Ranger), Lyris (Weaver)  
**Sessions:** 2  
**Result:** VICTORY (Pale Heart destroyed, Thane Coldbrook defeated)  

---

## Executive Summary

The Wintermarch Vigil is **well-balanced for 4 Level 1 characters** using the three Starter Guide classes. The adventure's three-encounter escalation (Easy → Deadly → Extreme) provides a complete mechanical tutorial that tests every core system. The party won with razor-thin margins in the final fight — exactly where a climactic encounter should land.

**The adventure works.** Below are specific findings.

---

## Mechanical Findings

### 1. Armor Attrition Curve

| After... | Tormund | Kessa | Sera | Lyris |
|----------|---------|-------|------|-------|
| Enc 1 | Full | Full | Full | Full |
| Enc 2 R3 | 0/0 | 0/0 | Full | Full (magic) |
| Recover | Full | Full | Full | Full |
| Enc 3 R3 | 0/shield | 0/0 | 1d8 | 1d12 |
| Enc 3 R6 | 0/0 | 0/0 | 0 | 0 |

**Finding:** Medium armor (2d8s) lasts 2-3 rounds against sustained attacks. Shields (1d12 at 67% survive) outlast armor by 2-3 rounds. This is correct — armor is an ablative buffer, not permanent protection.

**Recommendation:** No changes. Armor attrition rate drives the recovery/resource loop effectively.

### 2. Defense Capacity 1 at Level 1

With DC 1, every attack is "roll 1 die, pray." Against multi-HD attacks (Thane's 2 HD Cold Bolt, Logger's 2 HD Frozen Axe), a single failed defense = lose 2 dice. This creates rapid resource depletion.

**Finding:** DC 1 makes Level 1 combat feel dangerous and fast. Correct for the "fragile adventurers" feel of early play. Higher DC at Level 3+ will smooth this curve.

### 3. Shield Value

The 1d12 shield was the single most valuable defense item. Its 67% survive rate (7+) vs d8's 50% (5+) meant shields survived ~30% more incoming attacks. Both Combatants' shields were the last armor piece to fall, buying critical extra rounds.

**Finding:** Shield value is correctly calibrated. The Combatant's shield access is a genuine class advantage.

### 4. War-Trained Kill Trigger

Tormund and Kessa's kill triggers restored 1 HD each at critical moments. Tormund healed back to full from killing a Thrall in Round 7 of Session 2. This was tactically significant — without it, he'd have died to the Thane's Cold Bolt.

**Finding:** Kill trigger is a well-balanced sustain mechanic. Encourages aggressive play ("kill the trash to heal") rather than turtling.

### 5. Ranger's Nature's Treaty

Auto-succeed on Basic Wilderness checks made Sera the undisputed travel leader. Every Navigation check had 1 guaranteed success, reducing the party's failure rate from ~17% to ~5%.

**Finding:** Correct power level. Rangers SHOULD trivialize wilderness navigation. It's their class identity.

### 6. Weaver's Magical Armor

Lyris's pre-combat ritual (2d12s magic armor) gave her better armor than the medium-armored characters. d12 at 5+ = 67% survive vs d8 at 5+ = 50%. However, once destroyed, it cannot be renewed mid-combat.

**Finding:** Magical Armor is correctly powerful but fragile in long fights. The ritual requirement (10 min) prevents mid-combat refresh. Balance: strong opening, vulnerable late.

### 7. Overcast (1/long rest)

Lyris used Overcast to deal 3 HD with a single Curator's Bolt (normally 1 HD). This was used on the Frost Wall — tactically sound but not fight-ending.

**Finding:** Overcast provided meaningful burst but wasn't overpowered. 3 HD once per long rest is the right power level.

## Issues Requiring Attention

### CRITICAL

**Issue 1: Wraith Invulnerability to Physical Damage**  
Over 12 rounds of Session 2, the party dealt **0 total damage** to Wraiths. Incorporeal (halve physical) + Prof 2 defense (2d8 at 5+) = 2 HD halved to 1, then 56% chance to block. Physical attackers literally cannot damage them.

**Impact:** Any party without a Weaver (or similar force/magic damage source) auto-loses to Wraiths.

**Recommended Fixes (pick one):**
1. Add blessed arrows/weapons to Captain Frostfield's quest supplies
2. Give Wraiths vulnerability: "Takes full damage from force, divine, or fire"
3. Reduce Wraith defense to Prof 1 (1d8) so halved physical has a chance

### HIGH

**Issue 2: Lore Check at Burial Cairn (Expert 4+, need 3)**  
Mathematically impossible at Level 1 with 1-2d6. Max possible successes from 2d6 at 4+ = if both are 6 (2 each) = 4, but probability is ~0.3%. Need 3 at Expert from 2d6 requires at least one 6 for 2 successes + one 4+ for 1 = ~5.5%.

**Fix:** Change to Expert 4+, need 2 (achievable ~25% with exertion) or make it a Group Roll.

### MODERATE

**Issue 3: Frost Breath "2d6 cold damage" notation**  
Ambiguous — should be "2 HD cold damage" per Affinity's flat damage model. The "2d6" notation implies rolling dice for variable damage, which doesn't exist in the system.

**Fix:** Rewrite as "2 HD cold damage (half on successful Reflex save, Basic 2+/1)."

**Issue 4: Healing Draught Wording**  
"Restore 1d6 HD each" is ambiguous. Does 1d6 mean "variable 1-6 HD" or "1 Hit Die"?

**Fix:** Rewrite as "Restore 1 HD each" or "Roll 1d6: on 4+, restore 2 HD; on 1-3, restore 1 HD."

**Issue 5: Watch Bonfire "+1d6 HD overnight"**  
Same ambiguity as healing draughts.

**Fix:** Rewrite as "+1 HD overnight while campfire maintained."

**Issue 6: Hold the Line — Self-Benefit?**  
Text says "allies in zone." Does the caster count as their own ally? Needs explicit ruling.

**Fix:** Add "(including yourself)" or "(excluding yourself)" — suggest including for simplicity.

### LOW

**Issue 7: Encounter 1 Frost Aura negated by Cold Resistance**  
Frost Aura (1 cold/round) is completely nullified by the Winterwatch Blessing. The mechanic does nothing.

**Observation:** This is probably intentional (blessing counters the adventure's environmental damage). But GMs might wonder why the Aura exists. Add a GM note: "The blessing negates Frost Aura — this is intentional. Players won't face this protection in later adventures."

**Issue 8: Encounter 3 runs 8-12+ rounds**  
The climactic fight is a long slog. At a real table with 4 players + GM, each round is 5-7 minutes → 40-80 minutes per session for the final fight alone.

**Observation:** Not necessarily a bug — climactic fights should be long. But GMs should be warned.

**Fix:** Add a GM note: "This encounter is designed to run 8-12 rounds. If players seem bogged down, have the crystal develop visible cracks at 10 HP and 5 HP to show progress."
