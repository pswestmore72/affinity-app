## Session 2 Mechanical Observations

### What Worked Well

1. **Frost Wall** created dynamic tactical decisions. The Thane intelligently protected his crystal, forcing the party to split attention between wall-breaking and combat.
2. **Crystal-as-phylactery** (2 HD regen/round) made the fight feel hopeless until it broke. Classic TTRPG design — "kill the magic thing first."
3. **Incorporeal Wraiths** were appropriately terrifying. Physical characters couldn't touch them. This drives home: "bring the right tools for the job" and valorizes the Weaver's force damage.
4. **Frost Nova** as a desperation move was dramatic. Kessa dropping to 0 was the session's climactic moment.
5. **Thane's Prof 3 defense** (3d6 at 5+ = ~1 cancel per roll) made him feel like a boss. At 1-2 HD attacks, he shrugged off most damage.
6. **Crystal death shockwave** was a satisfying payoff. Thralls auto-dying and most Wraiths collapsing felt like "the tide turning."
7. **War-Trained kill trigger** kept Tormund in the fight when he needed it most.

### Issues Found

1. **Wraith Invulnerability:** Physical characters literally cannot damage Wraiths through Incorporeal + Prof 2 defense. 2 HD halved to 1, then almost always blocked by 2d8 at 5+ (56% to block 1 HD). Over 12 rounds, the party dealt **0 total damage to Wraiths.** This is too extreme — Wraiths are effectively immune to everything except force damage at Level 1.
   - **FIX:** Either give alchemical fire "half-bypass Incorporeal" (silver-weapon equivalent) or add a vulnerability: "Vulnerable to divine/force: takes full damage."

2. **Thane Regeneration + Frost Wall combo:** With 2 HD/round regen (including armor) and the ability to rebuild walls, the Thane is essentially unkillable while the crystal stands. The party has to destroy the wall (10 HP), THEN the crystal (20 HP), while also fighting the Thane + Wraiths + Thralls. That's 30 HP of objects at 1-2 HD per action = 15-30 actions = many rounds. The encounter lasted 12 rounds — this is long for a TTRPG table.
   - **OBSERVATION:** This isn't necessarily a problem. The encounter felt tense throughout. But GMs should know it'll run 8-12+ rounds.

3. **Frost Nova "3 HD cold" damage:** Against a character with no armor (Kessa at 3d10s, DC 1), 3 HD with a failed save = instant down. Roll 1 defense → fail → 1 die gone + 2 auto-destroy = all 3 dice consumed. This feels correct but VERY punishing. Players need to know: **maintain your armor.**

4. **Kessa at 1 HD for most of Session 2:** After the Frost Nova, Kessa was essentially a bystander. She could Rally (which was useful!) but couldn't fight. This is an interesting emergent gameplay moment — the "wounded ally" who supports from behind. But it also means 25% of the party was non-functional for 6+ rounds.

5. **Healing Draught Economy:** 3 draughts for a 2-session adventure is tight but workable. The party used all 3 (2 in Session 1, 1 in Session 2). If they'd used more in Session 1, Session 2 would've been significantly harder.
   - **RECOMMENDATION:** Consider 4-5 draughts for a 4-person party.

6. **Encounter Length:** Session 2's boss fight ran 12 rounds. At a real table with 4 players, that's 45-70 minutes of combat. Acceptable for a climactic battle but GMs should be warned. Consider adding: "This encounter is designed to run 8-12 rounds."

7. **Wraith Resolve Checks (forced on hit):** The mechanic exists but RT never escalated in this playtest (minimum RT 2). At RT 2, even a roll of 2 passes. This mechanic would only matter at higher RTs from prolonged fighting — but the party Recovered between sessions. If they'd Rested instead (RT minimum 2, no change), the Resolve checks would still auto-pass. **Wraith Resolve pressure only matters at higher levels or in longer adventuring days.**

8. **Magical Armor duration:** Lyris lost her magical armor during combat. She can't recast it mid-fight (ritual = 10 minutes). Once it's gone, she's at 4d6s with zero protection. This is working as designed — Weavers are glass cannons. But it emphasizes that the Weaver needs frontline protection.

## Adventure Verdict

### BALANCE RATING: CHALLENGING BUT FAIR

The Wintermarch Vigil is **correctly balanced for 4 Level 1 characters** with the following caveats:

| Encounter | Rating | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Frostbitten Scouts (Enc 1) | Easy | Tutorial fight. Zero risk. |
| Burial Cairn (Enc 2) | Deadly | Armor stripped fast. Crystal creates time pressure. Winnable. |
| Pale Heart (Enc 3) | Extreme | 12-round slog. One PC dropped to 0. Party won with 2-3 HD body each remaining. |

**Overall Difficulty Curve:** Easy → Deadly → Extreme. This is the correct escalation for a two-session starter adventure. Players learn combat in Enc 1, face real danger in Enc 2, and fight for survival in Enc 3.

### Would a TPK Have Occurred?
If Sera hadn't broken the crystal in Round 8, the party would likely have lost in Rounds 10-11. Tormund was burning 2 HD/round. Crystal regen was outpacing damage. The wall rebuilt once — if the Thane had used a third (if he had 3 charges instead of 2), the party would be dead. **The 2-wall limit is critical to balance.**

### Party Composition Notes
- **2 Combatants + 1 Ranger + 1 Weaver** is a solid party composition. The Combatants tanked, the Ranger provided mobility and damage, the Weaver provided utility (Ward, Bolt).
- **Without the Weaver:** The party would have no force damage. Wraiths would be literally immune. If a party has zero magic, the Wraiths are unbeatable.
  - **FIX:** Give Captain Frostfield "blessed arrows" (deal force damage to undead) as a quest reward. Ensures any party composition can handle incorporeal enemies.
